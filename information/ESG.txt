Price fluctuation happens due to:

Market Participation Cycles: These create seasonal price of volatility.
Technological Advancements: Cheaper solar panels and higher-capacity turbines affect generation cost and market behavior.

SEBI Integrates Green Credit Program into BRSR Core Framework
The Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) has recently introduced significant enhancements to its Business Responsibility and Sustainability Reporting (BRSR) framework by integrating the Green Credit Program (GCP). This initiative aims to encourage listed companies and their value chain partners to engage in environmentally sustainable activities and transparently disclose their contributions.
Background on BRSR Framework


Introduced in 2021, the BRSR framework mandates the top 1,000 listed entities in India to report their Environmental, Social, and Governance (ESG) performance. This reporting aligns with the nine principles outlined in the National Guidelines on Responsible Business Conduct (NGRBCs), facilitating standardized and comparable ESG disclosures.
Introduction of the Green Credit Program


In 2023, the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEFCC) launched the Green Credit Program through the Green Credit Rules, 2023. The program encompasses eight activities aimed at environmental preservation and protection, extending beyond mere greenhouse gas emissions reduction. Notably, on February 22, 2024, MoEFCC detailed the methodology for calculating green credits specifically for tree plantation activities.
Integration into BRSR Framework


Aligning with MoEFCC's initiative, SEBI has incorporated the Green Credit Program into the BRSR framework by introducing a leadership indicator under Principle 6, which emphasizes businesses' responsibility to protect and restore the environment. This addition requires listed entities to disclose the number of green credits generated or procured by the company and its top-10 value chain partners.
Implications for Listed Entities


The integration of the Green Credit Program into the BRSR framework has several implications for listed companies:
Enhanced Environmental Accountability: Companies are now encouraged to actively participate in environmentally sustainable activities, such as tree plantations, to generate green credits.
Transparent Reporting: The mandatory disclosure of green credits fosters transparency, allowing stakeholders to assess a company's commitment to environmental sustainability.
Incentivization of Value Chain Partners: By extending reporting requirements to significant value chain partners, companies can promote sustainable practices throughout their supply and distribution networks.
Redefinition of Value Chain Partners


To streamline compliance, SEBI has redefined 'value chain partners' to include upstream and downstream entities that individually account for 2% or more of the company's purchases or sales by value. This adjustment aims to reduce the compliance burden on smaller partners while ensuring that significant contributors are encompassed in ESG disclosures.
Transition from Assurance to Assessment


Recognizing the potential financial and compliance challenges associated with 'assurance' of ESG data, SEBI has proposed substituting the term 'assurance' with 'assessment' within the BRSR Core disclosures. This change offers companies the flexibility to undertake either an assessment or assurance of their ESG data, balancing rigor with practicality.

Conclusion


SEBI's integration of the Green Credit Program into the BRSR framework represents a significant step toward promoting environmental sustainability within India's corporate sector. By mandating the disclosure of green credits and redefining value chain reporting requirements, SEBI aims to enhance transparency, accountability, and sustainable practices among listed entities and their partners.

International Biodiversity Day


Introduction

It serves as a reminder of the critical importance of biodiversity in sustaining life on Earth. 
We celebrate this day to take action to protect and restore biodiversity. Biodiversity, the incredible variety of living organisms that inhabit our planet, is the foundation upon which human existence and well-being depend. 

What is Biodiversity ?

Biodiversity, a term coined by Walter G. Rosen in 1985, refers to the variety of life on Earth and the natural patterns it forms.
It encompasses the diversity within species, between species, and of ecosystems. Biodiversity is essential for ecosystem functionality and human survival, as it provides critical services such as food, clean water, medicine, and climate regulation. 
Despite its importance, biodiversity is facing rapid decline due to human activities, posing a severe threat to both nature and humanity.

Themes for International Biodiversity Day

The theme for 2024, Be Part of the Plan, emphasizes the need for a collective and coordinated approach to address .
Reverse the loss of biodiversity and supporting the implementation of the Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework.

Origin and Significance

The primary goal behind this initiative was to champion the preservation and sustainable utilization of the diversity in our planet.
This annual celebration serves as a platform to shine a spotlight on the crucial role that biodiversity plays in upholding the delicate equilibrium of ecosystems. 
By underscoring the importance of united conservation efforts, the International Day for Biological Diversity seeks to motivate individuals, communities, and nations to step up and protect the rich tapestry of life that sustains our planet.

History of the International Day for Biological Diversity
The International Day for Biological Diversity was established by the Second Committee of the UN General Assembly in 1993.
It was initially celebrated on December 29 to mark the day the Convention on Biological Diversity came into effect.
In December 2000, the UN General Assembly shifted the observance to May 22.
May 22 holds special significance as it marks the adoption of the Convention on Biological Diversity at the Rio de Janeiro Earth Summit in 1992.

Significance of the International Day for Biological Diversity
The significance of the International Day for Biological Diversity is profound, as it aligns with the UN Post-2015 Development Agenda’s Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs). Biodiversity is a cornerstone for sustainable development, impacting various global issues such as:
Oceans and Seas: Biodiversity in marine ecosystems supports fisheries, tourism, and coastal protection.
Forests: Forest biodiversity is crucial for ecosystem services, including carbon sequestration and oxygen production.
Food Security: Biodiversity in agriculture ensures the availability of a variety of crops, enhancing food security and nutrition.
Health: Many medicines are derived from plants and animals, making biodiversity vital for medical research and pharmaceuticals.
Sustainable Development: Biodiversity supports resilience against natural disasters and contributes to sustainable urban planning.
Desertification and Land Degradation: Biodiversity helps prevent land degradation and desertification, maintaining soil fertility and agricultural productivity.
Water and Sanitation: Healthy ecosystems regulate water cycles, ensuring clean and sustainable water supplies.
